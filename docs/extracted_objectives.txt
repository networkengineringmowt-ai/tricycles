SCOPE OF WORK (SOW)
Analysis of Tricycle Passenger Car Unit Values for Enhanced Traffic Flow in Kampala City, Uganda
Candidate: Sserunjogi Ambrose (Reg No: 2022-08-12043)
Institution: Kampala International University, School of Engineering and Applied Sciences
Prepared for: Technical Consultant Review
1. Core Research Objectives
To determine static Passenger Car Unit (PCU) values for passenger and cargo tricycles at selected Kampala intersections, targeting a precision of ±0.1 PCU.
To compare the derived tricycle PCU values against local values for motorcycles (boda-bodas) and passenger cars to quantify their relative impact on saturation flow.
To model the influence of dynamic factors—specifically congestion level (V/C ratio), time of day, and wet/dry pavement conditions—on tricycle PCU estimates.
2. Strategic Research Coupling Framework
To successfully deliver on the three research objectives, the field execution framework splits data acquisition into distinct but simultaneous components:
Objective 1 relies on clean headway pairs (Car-Car, Tricycle-Tricycle, Tricycle-Car) extracted via high-definition frame-by-frame video logging under free-flowing conditions.
Objective 2 requires macroscopic lane flow counts across all competing classifications to calculate comparative stream friction and baseline saturation limits.
Objective 3 demands a continuous 20-day time-series registration tracking peak-to-off-peak transitions, geometric layout variances (e.g., roundabout vs. signalized grid), and weather friction parameters (wet vs. dry pavement matrices).
3. Comprehensive Field Operation & Manpower Specification
Due to Kampala's extreme traffic heterogeneity, lack of lane compliance, and heavy pedestrian overlaps, automated sensors (such as pneumatic tubes) are prone to occlusion and counting errors. Therefore, manual enumerators paired with overhead high-definition cameras form the technical core of this study. Enumerators will be strategically deployed at specific cordon lines across the 5 high-density study locations.
3.1 Enumerator Deployment Matrix per Direction
To prevent data loss at complex geometric nodes, enumerators are assigned per inbound approach leg. A standard 4-leg intersection with 4 discrete directions requires a baseline layout of 16 enumerators (4 per approach leg: 1 capturing small agile motorized classes like motorcycles and tricycles, and 1 dedicated to heavy, medium, and passenger cars). Kibuye Roundabout requires an expanded deployment due to 5 interacting approach corridors and severe cross-weaving conflicts.

3.2 Sampling Strategy, Rotation Schedules, and Shift Structures
To capture Kampala's irregular seasonal shifts and fulfill Objective 3, field data collection runs over 20 consecutive days. Observations operate on an 10-hour continuous daily cycle with the first window at (07:00am – 11:00am) and the other at (04:00pm – 10:00pm) to capture peak operational hours.
The activity will be held over 2 weekdays, namely Thursday and Friday, and 1 weekend day, namely Saturday. We can also operate on Monday, Tuesday and Sunday to ease the work.
